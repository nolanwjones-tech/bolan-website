Perfect Day Together

For wedding planners

Help your couples work it out themselves, before it becomes your problem.

You know the moment: a couple can't agree on the guest list, the budget or how involved the in-laws get, and suddenly you're the referee. Perfect Day Together gives them a private, calm way to work it through on their own, so you can get back to planning.

What you see: the topic (e.g. "Guest list") and where it stands: waiting, reviewing alternatives, or resolved. What stays private: everything they write, the alternatives offered, and any recommendation. Conversations are automatically deleted after 90 days.

Try it with one couple: perfectdaytogether.comQuestions? support@perfectdaytogether.com

How it works
  1. Send a private link. Create a conversation from your dashboard and send it to your couple. They don't need an account or an app.
  2. They each share their side. Privately, in their own words, or with "Keep it Cool," where each hears a calm, neutral summary of the other's position.
  3. Alternatives come first. They see 2–4 concrete options neither of them proposed. Most disagreements are settled right here.
  4. A recommendation, only if needed. If nothing fits, either of them can request one, non-binding and in the tone you choose.
What it looks like

Guest list. One partner wants to stay near 100 guests to protect the budget; the other wants all 180 relatives invited. The first alternative offered: "Ceremony for All, Reception for Fewer." Invite everyone to the ceremony, where per-person cost is small, and host a smaller, nicer reception within budget. No recommendation needed.

Suggestions are non-binding and are not legal, financial, or professional advice. Intended for low-stakes wedding-planning disagreements.

Restaurant Social Engagement

For restaurants and venues

End the argument. Get to your restaurant.

Every night, groups argue about who's driving, who's buying and where to eat, usually around 1 AM, usually in a group chat. Restaurant Social Engagement turns that moment into a fun, shareable ruling from a judge with your brand's attitude, and every ruling ends at your door.

  • Your name and look. A version of the site branded as yours, on its own web address.
  • A judge with your voice. Blunt, grandmotherly, over-the-top courtroom, or one written just for your brand.
  • A closing line you control. A free side, a check-in, a hashtag, a happy-hour reminder. You pick the offer.
  • Nothing for you to run. No staff time, no app to maintain. We handle the hosting and upkeep.

Try the restaurant app

How it works for your guests
  1. One person describes the argument. No app and no login.
  2. They send a link to the other person, who adds their side.
  3. The judge calls it in about a minute. Fast, funny, non-binding, and it closes with your line.
What a ruling looks like

Marcus says it's Dev's turn to drive. Dev says he always has to pick the spot.

The Case of the Reluctant Driver: "Dev drives. Marcus buys, and doesn't complain about it. Navigating to a place you've been to forty times is not a real job. Go."

Rulings are lighthearted, non-binding, and not legal, financial, or professional advice.

Verdict

For YouTube creators

Turn your personality into a judge your fans can use.

Your audience already loves how you joke, react and call things out. This turns that into something fans can use: they bring a small disagreement and get a fast, funny, non-binding ruling in your voice, ending with your sign-off.

  • A judge voiced from you. Built from the traits your fans already know from your videos.
  • A sign-off you control. A catchphrase, a link, or a sponsor mention at the end of every ruling.
  • Your name and look. Branded as yours, on its own web address.
  • Shareable rulings. Each one is its own page with a preview that fans can post.

Try the creator app

How it works for your fans
  1. A fan describes the disagreement. Chores, a split bill, whose turn it is. No login.
  2. They send a link to the other person, who adds their side.
  3. They get a ruling in about a minute, in your style.
What a ruling looks like

One roommate always cleans the brushes. The other says hauling lumber is the harder job.

The Case of the Abandoned Paint Brushes: "Whoever opens the can owns the brush 'til it's clean, no exceptions, no 'soaking' loopholes. Hauling lumber is real work, but it's not a free pass on brush duty."

Rulings are lighthearted, non-binding, and not legal, financial, or professional advice. The judge's voice is built with your input and approval.

Fair Enough

For friends, roommates, couples and coworkers

Settle the argument. Stay friends.

Fair Enough is a free web app where two people each say their side of a small disagreement, and an impartial AI referee weighs both sides and suggests a fair, non-binding resolution in about a minute. No login required.

  • No login, no email. Access is through a private link you share only with the person you're disagreeing with.
  • "Keep it Cool" mode. Neither side sees the other's exact words. Each person gets a calm, neutral summary of the other's position instead.
  • Five mediator styles. Balanced, Straight Shooter, The Grandmother, The Sportscaster or Dramatic Courtroom. Same fair decision underneath; you choose how it's delivered.
  • Shareable results. Each resolution has a name-free public page that previews well when posted. The full ruling stays private to the two people.
  • Delete anytime. Either person can permanently delete a case. Everything else is automatically deleted after 90 days.

Start a dispute: getfairenough.comQuestions? support@getfairenough.com

How it works
  1. Describe your side of the disagreement.
  2. Send the link you get to the other person so they can add theirs.
  3. Get a fair resolution. We ask a clarifying question or two if needed, then suggest a fair resolution, usually within a minute.
What a result looks like

Sam says they always unload the dishwasher and Riley starts it and disappears. Riley says they do the scrubbing of pots that don't fit, which is the worse job.

The Matter of the Abandoned Dishwasher: Whoever starts the dishwasher owns it end to end, including unloading it the next morning. Pots and pans that need hand-washing are the other person's job that same night. Neither of them gets to start a load and walk away.

For small, low-stakes disagreements between people who basically get along. Not for anything involving safety, harassment, money that really matters, or legal questions. Fair Enough is a lighthearted tool for small disagreements. Its suggestions are non-binding and are not legal, financial, or professional advice.
